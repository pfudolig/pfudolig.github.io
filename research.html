VaDAR

Dual Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) are important observational tools for studying the dynamical evolution of supermassive black holes and their host galaxies. Yet, their detection is notoriously difficult due to current observational limits. Varstrometry for Dual AGN using Radio Interferometry is a project that leverages the exquisite positional accuracy of the Gaia space telescope with the high spatial resolution of radio interferometers (i.e. the Very Large Array and the Very Long Baseline Array) to detect astrometrically variable quasars as a method of searching for dual AGN candidates.

Publications

Radio Emission in Radio-Quiet Quasars

Radio-quiet quasars make up approximately 90% of the quasar population, yet require much higher sensitivity and spatial resolution to be detected compared to their brighter radio-loud counterparts. As a result, characterizations of the radio-quiet quasar class are ongoing. With this project, we study how different physical mechanisms and photometric variabilities manifest in radio-quiet quasars, using data from the Very Large Array and Very Long Baseline Array.
